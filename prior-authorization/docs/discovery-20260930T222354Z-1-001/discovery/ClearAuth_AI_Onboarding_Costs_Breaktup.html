Onboarding Costs

I’m assuming your $50 means $50 per onboarding/implementation hour, not $50 per prior authorization. Charging $50 per PA would be difficult to justify against the attached benchmark of $20–$30 manual cost per PA.

Based on the architecture we defined and your attached volume/cost assumptions, I would price onboarding mostly by integration complexity, not physician count. A 10-doctor practice does not take 10× the onboarding effort of a 1-doctor practice because the EHR, payer routes, n8n workflows, security, and portal integrations are practice-level setup.

The client requirement supports this model: ClearAuth receives patient/insurance/CPT/diagnosis/order data, checks electronically first, uses payer/clearinghouse or portal integrations, gathers clinical documents, and routes only exceptions to humans.

Recommended onboarding formula

Use this formula:

Onboarding Hours = 34 + (0.25 × Physicians) + (3 × Electronic Payer Routes) + (4 × Portal Routes)

The fixed 34 hours covers the common work:

Onboarding activityHours
Discovery + access readiness4
Practice/tenant/security setup4
EHR integration + data mapping10
n8n/HITL workflow configuration4
End-to-end UAT6
User/admin training2
Go-live + initial hypercare4
Base hours34

Then add:

  • 0.25 hr per physician for provider/NPI/EHR mapping
  • 3 hrs per electronic payer/clearinghouse route
  • 4 hrs per payer portal needing browser configuration/validation

So:

Onboarding Cost = Onboarding Hours × $50/hour

I would then add 15% implementation contingency for credential issues, EHR field differences, test-data problems, payer portal changes, etc.

Example: 10-physician practice

Assume:

  • 10 physicians
  • 1 already-supported EHR
  • 3 electronic payer/clearinghouse routes
  • 2 payer portals requiring browser fallback

Calculation:

34 base hours

  • 10 × 0.25 = 2.5 hours physician mapping
  • 3 × 3 = 9 hours electronic payer setup
  • 2 × 4 = 8 hours portal setup
= 53.5 onboarding hours

At $50/hour:

53.5 × $50 = $2,675

With 15% contingency:

$2,675 × 1.15 = $3,076

Recommendation

For a normal 10-physician practice with an already-supported EHR, I would offer:

ClearAuth Standard Onboarding: $3,000–$3,500 one time

That is cleaner commercially than invoicing 53.5 hours.

Now compare that to the client's current PA burden

Your attached numbers say a physician handles roughly:

160–175 PAs/month

Therefore a 10-physician practice has:

1,600–1,750 PAs/month

The attached manual-cost assumption is:

$20–$30 per PA

So current administrative burden is approximately:

Low end

1,600 × $20 = $32,000/month

High end

1,750 × $30 = $52,500/month

Current PA administrative burden ≈ $32,000–$52,500/month

What automation potentially saves

Your attached data says automation can reduce manual PA work from 20+ minutes to under 5 minutes.

For modeling, that is roughly a:

75% reduction in handling time

If cost scaled proportionally, the theoretical administrative value automated would be:

Low end

$32,000 × 75% = $24,000/month

High end

$52,500 × 75% = $39,375/month

So the theoretical gross savings opportunity for a 10-physician practice is:

$24,000–$39,375/month

I would not sell using this entire number because some authorizations will still need human intervention, peer-to-peer review, missing-document resolution, portal failures, etc. The client requirement itself specifically retains humans for medical-necessity questions, peer-to-peer reviews, and unusual denials.

For a business case, use a more conservative 50% realization of that theoretical value initially:

Estimated practical savings: $12,000–$19,688/month

That makes the sales story much more defensible.

Payback on onboarding

If onboarding costs approximately $3,100:

At $12,000/month savings:

$3,100 ÷ $12,000 = 0.258 months

Approximately 7–8 days to recover onboarding cost.

At $19,688/month savings:

$3,100 ÷ $19,688 = 0.157 months

Approximately 5 days.

So even under the conservative model:

A 10-physician practice could recover the ClearAuth onboarding cost within approximately one week of operational savings.

Again, that is before recurring ClearAuth subscription fees, API/clearinghouse fees, browser/voice costs, or support costs.

Onboarding cost grows slowly, while the customer's value grows almost linearly with PA volume.

That is why I would not price onboarding per physician.

Better pricing model

I would structure ClearAuth commercially in three pieces.

1. One-time onboarding

For a normal practice with supported integrations:

$3,000–$3,500

Includes:

  • practice setup
  • doctor mapping
  • EHR configuration
  • payer route configuration
  • n8n setup
  • portal configuration
  • HITL setup
  • UAT
  • training
  • go-live

2. Net-new integration charges

Do not absorb custom integration work into the standard onboarding price.

For example:

Additional workSuggested formula at $50/hr
New EHR integration20–40 hrs = $1,000–$2,000
New electronic payer integration8–16 hrs = $400–$800
New payer portal automation8–16 hrs = $400–$800
Voice payer workflow8–20 hrs = $400–$1,000
Additional complex location/workflow4–8 hrs = $200–$400

Once a connector becomes reusable, later customers should not incur the full development cost.

That is where your margins improve significantly.

The formula I would actually put into the business plan

Onboarding

H = 34 + 0.25P + 3E + 4B + N

Where:

  • P = number of physicians
  • E = electronic payer connections
  • B = browser/portal payer connections
  • N = net-new integration/customization hours

Then:

Onboarding Cost = H × $50

And:

Quoted Onboarding Price = Onboarding Cost × 1.15

for standard contingency.

Customer savings

Use:

Monthly Manual Cost = Monthly PA Volume × Manual Cost per PA

Then:

Theoretical Automation Value = Monthly Manual Cost × Automation Reduction

With your numbers:

Automation Reduction ≈ 75%

For conservative sales modeling:

Expected Savings = Theoretical Automation Value × 50% realization factor

So the complete formula becomes:

Expected Monthly Savings = PA Volume × $20–$30 × 75% × 50%

For the 10-physician example:

1,600–1,750 × $20–$30 × 0.75 × 0.50

= approximately:

$12,000–$19,688/month

My recommended commercial position

For the first customers, I would use:

Standard ClearAuth Onboarding: $3,000

and define it as:

1 practice, up to 10 physicians, one supported EHR, up to three supported electronic payer routes, two payer portal configurations, HITL workflow, training and go-live.

Then clearly state:

Net-new EHR or payer integrations are estimated separately at $50/hour.

That protects you from a customer whose EHR unexpectedly requires 50 hours of custom work.

More importantly, don't lead the customer with “we spent 54 hours onboarding you.” Lead with:

“Your practice currently processes approximately 1,600–1,750 prior authorizations every month. At the stated $20–$30 administrative burden per authorization, that represents $32K–$52.5K of monthly burden. A one-time ~$3K ClearAuth onboarding investment is small relative to even a conservative $12K–$20K/month automation opportunity.”

That is the much stronger ROI story.
