What is Medblocks?

Medblocks is the integration layer between your product and the healthcare systems that hold patient data. It helps you reach the systems your relationship allows, complete authorization, pull records in the background, and send those records to the systems where your team works.

How does it work?

It has five important layers. Each one hands off to the next, so the easiest way to hold the whole thing is as a single line running left to right.

1

Source

Where healthcare data comes from

In Medblocks, a Source simply means: the place where healthcare data comes from.

Think of it like this — start with the patient's data, ask where it is stored, and whatever system answers that question is the Source.

Examples of sources

Epic EHR Oracle Health (Cerner) athenahealth eClinicalWorks Insurance payer systems CMS Blue Button Wearables — Apple Watch, Fitbit Patient portals HIE networks — Carequality, CommonWell

Medblocks calls all of these Sources.

A simple real-world example

Suppose a patient named John has data in a hospital running Epic, at his insurance company, and on his Apple Watch. For Medblocks, that is three sources — and it connects to all of them to gather the data.

2

Connection

The authorized link to a source

In Medblocks, a Connection is the authorized link between Medblocks and a Source.

Source
Where the healthcare data lives
Connection
Permissioned bridge to access that data
Think of it like this

Google Drive is the source. Logged-in access from your app is the connection.

Without a connection, your app knows the source exists but cannot access its data.

An easy example

Suppose a patient's records are in Epic. The patient logs in and grants permission, a connection is created, and only then can Medblocks access the records. Epic is the Source; the authorized access to Epic is the Connection.

Multiple sources, multiple connections

A patient may have data in many places at once — Epic, Quest Diagnostics, an insurance company, Apple Health. Medblocks creates a separate connection to each. Once connected, it can retrieve data from all of them.

3

Pull

Fetching the latest records

In Medblocks, a Pull means: go to a connected source and fetch the latest patient data.

A simple analogy

Think of Gmail on your phone. The Gmail server holds your mail; your phone checks for new email and downloads it. That action is a pull — your phone is pulling data from Gmail.

Medblocks does the same thing, with Epic in place of the Gmail server and a Connection in place of your signed-in account.

A real-world example

A patient visits a hospital today and receives a new diagnosis, a new lab result and a new prescription. Those updates now exist in Epic. When Medblocks runs a pull, the new lab result travels from Epic into Medblocks — and your application can see the latest information.

4

Updates

Knowing what happened

Your app receives operational updates through webhooks and dashboard history.

A pull happens in the background, so your app is not watching it. Webhooks are how Medblocks tells you when something changed; the dashboard is where a person goes to look at what already happened. Four events cover the things worth reacting to:

patient_session.completed
The patient finished the authorization flow
records.sync.completed
A background pull finished — new data is ready
records.sync.failed
A pull failed — worth alerting someone
connection.token_refresh_failed
Access lapsed — ask the patient to reconnect

That last one is the reason this layer exists at all. A connection that worked yesterday can quietly stop working today, and nothing in layers 1 to 3 would tell you.

5

Data out

Where the records end up

Records leave Medblocks through exports, API reads, or downstream workflows.

Three exits, chosen by what you are feeding. Exports keep another system in sync. API reads answer a question about one patient right now. Webhooks trigger a workflow the moment something lands.

The whole thing, in one sentence

Medblocks finds the system that holds a patient's data (Source), gets permission to reach it (Connection), fetches the records in the background (Pull), tells you what happened (Updates), and delivers the records to wherever your team works (Data out).

Everything else in the platform — the dashboards, the error codes, the sandboxes, the SDK — exists to make one of those five layers survive contact with a real hospital.
